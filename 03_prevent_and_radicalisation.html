Prevent is a safeguarding responsibility

Prevent aims to stop people becoming terrorists or supporting terrorism. In education and training, it sits within safeguarding. The purpose is early support, not punishment, and concerns should be handled by trained safeguarding staff.

No single sign proves radicalisation. Changes in behaviour can have many explanations. Avoid stereotypes, assumptions about identity and attempts to investigate someone yourself.

What may increase concern?

Exploitation of vulnerability

A person may be targeted when isolated, grieving, angry, searching for belonging, experiencing discrimination or struggling with change. Vulnerability does not mean inevitability.

Pressure and control

Concern may increase where a group demands secrecy, cuts someone off from others, discourages questions or pressures them to prove commitment.

Escalating harmful behaviour

Examples include repeated support for terrorist violence, attempts to obtain or share terrorist material, direct threats or movement from discussion towards action.

Context and pattern

Look at the whole picture. A lawful opinion, religious practice, identity, protest or difficult conversation is not a Prevent concern on its own.

The JPC response

Stay curious, not accusatory

Use calm, open questions only where appropriate. Do not debate, interrogate or try to catch someone out.

Record what is factual

Write down what you directly saw, heard or received, including dates and context. Avoid labels and assumptions.

Share through safeguarding

Use the designated safeguarding lead or named Prevent contact. They can decide whether advice, early support or a referral is appropriate.

Act immediately where necessary

If there is a direct threat, weapon, planned attack or immediate danger, use emergency procedures first.

UK scope

The statutory Prevent duty applies to specified authorities in England, Wales and Scotland, with separate guidance for Scotland. Apprenticeship and training providers may have specific responsibilities. Northern Ireland has separate safeguarding and counter-terrorism arrangements, so follow your provider's policy.
