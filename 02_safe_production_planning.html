Safeguarding starts in the schedule

Junior Production Coordinators often control the practical information that helps a production run safely. A call sheet, travel plan, room booking, contact list or late schedule change can either reduce risk or create it.

Think beyond children. Safe production planning also protects adult contributors, freelancers, apprentices, people with access needs, anyone working alone and people taking part in sensitive content.

The five-part production check

People

Age, role, access needs, vulnerability, supervision, power differences, welfare contacts and who has direct contact with contributors.

Place

Private spaces, changing areas, toilets, access, transport, isolated locations, public interaction and emergency arrangements.

Time

Working hours, breaks, late finishes, travel home, overnights, fatigue, schedule changes and check-in times.

Information

Who needs contact details, medical or access information, consent records, sensitive notes, footage and location data.

Power

Who can say no, who controls opportunities, whether participation is genuinely voluntary and how a concern can be raised without fear.

Build safeguards into normal production documents

Call sheet

Include a named welfare or safeguarding contact, safe travel information, suitable facilities and only the personal information people genuinely need.

Risk assessment

Cover lone working, contributor contact, sensitive content, location access, fatigue, harassment, online exposure and changes to the plan.

Schedule

Allow time for briefings, breaks, chaperone or licensing requirements, welfare checks, accessible travel and aftercare.

Contact plan

Use approved channels. Make escalation clear. Do not rely on one person, one phone or an informal group chat.
