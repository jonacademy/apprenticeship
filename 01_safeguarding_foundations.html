Why this matters in production

Safeguarding is not limited to productions involving children. A coordinator may notice concerns while arranging travel, booking contributors, handling contact details, preparing call sheets, monitoring working hours, supporting a distressed colleague or receiving information that was not intended as a formal disclosure.

Your role is not to prove what happened. Your role is to notice, respond safely, record facts and report through the correct route.

Who may need safeguarding support?

Children and young people

Anyone under 18. They might be a performer, contributor, apprentice, runner, audience member or visitor. Production rules can also include licensing, chaperones, working hours and suitable spaces.

Adults

Adults can experience abuse, neglect, exploitation, coercive control or self-neglect. The legal threshold and terminology vary across the UK, so use your organisation's policy rather than trying to make a legal judgement yourself.

A safe response in four steps

Listen

Stay calm. Give the person time. Do not press for unnecessary detail or ask leading questions.

Be clear

Thank them for telling you. Do not promise secrecy. Explain that you may need to share the concern with the right person.

Record facts

Write what you saw or heard, when it happened and the exact words used where possible. Keep opinion separate from fact.

Report promptly

Use the safeguarding lead, designated person, welfare contact or escalation route named by your employer or training provider.

Production situations to reveal

Booking and casting

Look for unclear consent, pressure to participate, direct contact with under-18s outside agreed channels, or a contributor who does not appear to understand what is being asked.

Travel and accommodation

Consider supervision, rooming arrangements, lone travel, late finishes, access needs, safe transport and who is responsible if plans change.

On set or in the office

Notice changes in behaviour, intimidation, unwanted contact, discriminatory treatment, unsafe isolation or a person who appears frightened of someone else.

After filming

Concerns can arise through messages, social media, edits, publicity, online abuse or the impact of transmission. Aftercare and a clear contact route matter.
