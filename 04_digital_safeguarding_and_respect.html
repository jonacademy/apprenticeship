Bullying, harassment and online harm

Digital choices can create real-world harm

Production work involves personal data, images, contributor stories, rushes, group chats, cloud folders, social media and user-generated content. Poor handling can expose people to unwanted contact, harassment, doxxing, discrimination, exploitation or distress.

Respectful workplace conduct is part of safety. Bullying, harassment and racism are not always labelled as safeguarding, but they can overlap with abuse, exploitation and serious welfare concerns.

The digital safeguarding check

Purpose

Why are you collecting, sharing or publishing this information? Is the use clear to the person involved?

Minimum access

Who genuinely needs the information or footage? Remove unnecessary contact details and restrict sensitive folders.

Consent and choice

Is participation freely agreed? Is the planned use specific? Can the person ask questions or raise concerns without pressure?

Context and impact

Could an edit, caption, location tag or publication expose someone, misrepresent them or cause foreseeable harm?

Report, do not amplify

Where content is harmful or potentially illegal, do not forward it. Use platform, safeguarding, legal and police routes as appropriate.

Workplace behaviour

Online messages, late-night chats and social media contact still need professional boundaries and clear reporting routes.

What a JPC can put in place

Approved channels

Keep contributor and crew contact on agreed systems. Avoid personal accounts unless there is a clear, approved reason.

Access control

Use role-based folders, secure links and expiry dates where available. Remove access when people leave the project.

Clear welfare route

Put the named welfare, safeguarding or dignity-at-work contact on onboarding information and call sheets.

Pause before publishing

Check permissions, context, captions, metadata, location clues and the possible impact on contributors or staff.
